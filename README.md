# Adaptive Cycle

> An open, bilingual exploration of adaptive personal mobility: human-powered, electrically assisted, accessible, and designed to preserve independence.

[Deutsch](README_DE.md) · [Documentation index](docs/README.md) · [Roadmap](docs/roadmap.md)

## About the project

Adaptive Cycle is a public research and design project for a configurable personal mobility platform. The project explores bicycle, tricycle, and light electric vehicle ideas without assuming that one layout will suit every rider or every journey.

The working persona **Ol’ Captain Hinkebein** helps us discuss real-world needs without identifying a specific person. It is a design aid, not a medical label or a definition of the intended users.

This repository begins as a structured notebook. Concepts documented here are proposals until they are supported by calculations, prototypes, tests, and—where relevant—professional legal or medical review.

## Design goals

- Preserve autonomy, dignity, and the pleasure of moving under one’s own power.
- Combine foot, hand, and electric propulsion where useful.
- Support stable access, mounting, riding, stopping, and parking.
- Adapt to changing abilities and different journey types.
- Keep safety, maintainability, and lawful road use visible throughout the design process.
- Publish research and decisions in both English and German.

## How to contribute

Start with the [documentation index](docs/README.md). Keep English and German counterparts linked, identify assumptions, cite primary sources, and record important decisions in the relevant document. Contributions may improve either language; translation parity is a project goal, not a barrier to participation.

## Safety and regulatory notice

This repository contains exploratory engineering and regulatory research, not certified designs, medical advice, or legal advice. Do not build or operate a vehicle solely from these notes. Safety-critical work requires appropriate analysis, testing, and qualified review. Rules vary by vehicle classification, configuration, place, and date; verify them with current primary sources.
