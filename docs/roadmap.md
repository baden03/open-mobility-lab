## Phase 1 — Needs and constraints

- Co-design representative journey and accessibility scenarios.
- Define configurations instead of treating the vehicle as one fixed object.
- Establish measurable requirements, assumptions, and a risk register.
- Build Germany/EU classification and standards research from current primary sources.

**Gate:** Agreed needs, configuration set, major hazards, and evidence plan.

## Phase 2 — Architecture and low-risk rigs

- Compare chassis and wheel layouts.
- Build adjustable, stationary biomechanics and rowing-drive rigs.
- Model stability, energy, steering, suspension, and structural load cases.
- Define mechanical, electrical, firmware, and CAD interfaces.

**Gate:** Architecture decision supported by measurements and safety review.

## Phase 3 — Rolling prototype

- Build a low-speed instrumented prototype.
- Verify braking, steering, stability, access, propulsion, faults, and emergency operation.
- Keep public-road testing outside scope until classification and safety prerequisites are met.

**Gate:** Documented verification against prototype requirements.

## Phase 4 — Iteration and release

- Test with representative riders under an approved protocol.
- Resolve failures and usability findings; repeat verification.
- Obtain qualified regulatory and safety review.
- Release reproducible design packages with configuration, limitations, and maintenance information.
